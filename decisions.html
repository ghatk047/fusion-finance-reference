Foundation

DD-01

Is voyage a chart of accounts segment, a PPM project, or a cost centre?

Voyage is the fundamental economic unit in cruise. This choice governs revenue attribution, cost allocation, close runtime, analytics and the shipboard integration design. It is the most consequential single decision in the programme and it is required before the chart of accounts can be frozen.

COA segment — Intuitive to finance, natively available on every transaction, but multiplies account combinations by annual voyage volume across the fleet and degrades allocation and close performance permanently.
PPM project — Handles high cardinality without inflating the COA, supports budget and actual by voyage, and carries cost naturally. Requires PPM in scope and project creation automation from the deployment schedule.
Cost centre — Lowest build effort but conflates organisational and voyage reporting, and cost centre hierarchies are not designed for objects that are created and closed weekly.
Our recommendation
PPM project for voyage, with vessel as a COA segment

Vessel is a stable, low-cardinality dimension that belongs in the COA. Voyage is high-cardinality and time-boxed, which is exactly what a project is for. This keeps the COA compact enough to close quickly while still producing voyage P&L, and it makes drydock and newbuild capital naturally consistent with voyage costing.

Decide byDesign — before COA freeze

Blocks until decidedChart of accounts design, subledger accounting rules, SPMS integration mapping, voyage P&L planning model

DD-02

Single group ledger, or a separate ledger per brand?

Ledger structure cannot be changed after transactions post without a re-implementation. It determines close mechanics, statutory reporting capability and how far brand results can diverge in presentation.

Single primary ledger — Simplest close, one calendar and accounting method, brand separation via balancing segment. Constrains brand-specific statutory presentation.
Ledger per brand — Full flexibility for Norwegian, Oceania and Regent, but triples close effort and complicates consolidation.
Single ledger plus secondary ledgers — One operational ledger with secondary ledgers where local GAAP or brand statutory reporting genuinely requires it.
Our recommendation
Single primary ledger with secondary ledgers where statutory need is proven

The three brands share a functional currency and accounting framework. Separating ledgers to solve a reporting problem that balancing segments already solve buys permanent close cost for no benefit. Secondary ledgers are the escape valve for genuine statutory divergence.

DD-03

How many COA segments, and what does each carry?

Segment count drives combination volume, close runtime, security complexity and reporting capability for the platform's whole life. Every stakeholder wants one more segment and each one compounds.

Lift the PeopleSoft structure — Fastest, lowest risk of surprise, but imports every existing limitation into a decade-long platform.
Redesign to 7-9 segments — Company, cost centre, natural account, vessel, brand, intercompany, plus one or two future-use. Balances capability against performance.
Minimal COA with dimensions elsewhere — Small COA, analysis carried in PPM, supporting references and Fusion Data Intelligence. Best performance, steepest change for users.
Our recommendation
Redesign to 7-9 segments, with voyage held outside the COA per DD-01

A re-implementation is the only opportunity to fix the COA for a decade. Lifting PeopleSoft wastes it. But every segment beyond about nine costs close performance for reporting most stakeholders will not actually use.

DD-04

Business units aligned to shared service, or to brand?

Business unit design scopes Payables, Receivables and Procurement processing, drives security, and determines whether shared services can process across brands without switching context.

Single shared-service BU per function — Cleanest for a shared service operating model, minimum security overhead.
BU per brand — Mirrors the current organisation but multiplies configuration and blocks cross-brand processing efficiency.
Our recommendation
Shared-service BU per function unless brand-level processing autonomy is a stated operating model requirement

BU proliferation inherited from legacy structures creates permanent security and reporting overhead that nobody ever unwinds. Design for the target operating model, not the current org chart.

Revenue

DD-05

Is passenger ticket revenue recognised at sail, or per diem across the voyage?

Under ASC 606 the performance obligation is satisfied as the voyage is delivered. Recognising at sail is simpler but overstates revenue in the sailing month for long itineraries and creates a period-end cliff.

Point recognition at sail — Simple, avoids the straddle problem, but is not defensible for Regent and Oceania itineraries running several weeks.
Per-diem across voyage days — Faithful to the obligation, requires a recognition schedule and a period-end straddle rule.
Our recommendation
Per diem across voyage days, with an explicit straddle rule per DD-06

Brand itinerary lengths differ materially. A single point-recognition policy that works for a 7-night Norwegian sailing is hard to defend on a Regent world cruise. Per diem is the same policy applied consistently across all three brands.

DD-06

How is revenue split for voyages straddling a period end?

At any month end a substantial portion of the fleet is mid-voyage. The straddle is the normal case, not an edge case, and the rule must be mechanical enough to apply at fleet scale inside the close window.

Calendar-day proration — Simple, auditable, applies uniformly. Ignores that embarkation and debarkation days differ in delivered value.
Weighted by voyage phase — More faithful to actual delivery but introduces judgement and is far harder to explain to auditors.
Recognise on completion — Avoids the split entirely but defers revenue in a way that distorts monthly results.
Our recommendation
Calendar-day proration, applied without exception

The value of a mechanical rule at fleet scale exceeds the precision gained from weighting. A rule that requires judgement on hundreds of voyages every month end will not survive contact with the close calendar.

DD-07

Where is the Future Cruise Credit liability held — Fusion, or the booking system?

FCCs are among the largest liabilities on a cruise balance sheet, are long-dated, and carry expiry, partial redemption and transferability at individual credit level. Whichever system holds the detail becomes the system of record for a material disclosure.

Fusion Receivables as system of record — Ledger-level detail with native ageing, but Fusion has no delivered FCC lifecycle model — this means an extension.
Booking system as system of record, summarised to Fusion — Uses existing capability, but ledger reconciliation depends entirely on the quality of the booking system extract.
Bespoke subledger via Accounting Hub — Full control of the lifecycle model with clean accounting, at the cost of building and owning a subledger.
Our recommendation
Booking system as system of record, with a detailed daily feed to Fusion via Accounting Hub

FCC issuance and redemption are commercial events that happen in the booking flow. Moving the system of record to finance fights the natural transaction path. But summarising to a single balance is not enough for disclosure or breakage — the feed must carry credit-level detail.

DD-08

What is the FCC breakage policy and estimation basis?

Breakage recognises income on credits that will never be redeemed. It requires historical redemption data, and the policy directly affects reported revenue. Applied without documented support it is a restatement risk.

Recognise only on expiry — Most conservative and simplest to defend, but defers income the business has economically earned.
Proportional recognition using historical redemption patterns — Matches expected experience but requires redemption history that may not survive migration.
No breakage recognised — Maximum conservatism; liability persists until expiry or redemption.
Our recommendation
Recognise on expiry initially, moving to proportional once post-migration redemption history supports it

Proportional breakage needs a redemption dataset the migration may fragment. Starting on expiry and moving to proportional when the data supports it is defensible in both directions and avoids a policy change driven by data loss.

DD-09

Are port fees and government taxes presented gross or net?

Per-passenger port charges are collected from the guest and remitted to authorities. The principal-versus-agent assessment determines whether they inflate reported revenue, and the answer can differ by charge type and jurisdiction.

Gross — revenue and cost — Higher reported revenue; requires NCLH to be principal for the service, which is arguable for some charges and not others.
Net — pass-through liability — Cleaner economics and a more conservative revenue position, but reduces headline revenue against peers who present gross.
Mixed by charge type — Most technically faithful, hardest to apply consistently across hundreds of ports.
Our recommendation
Net for genuine pass-through government charges; gross only where NCLH controls the service

The assessment should follow control, not comparability optics. But a mixed answer must be documented per charge type at design time — deciding case by case at the port level guarantees inconsistency and an audit finding.

DD-10

At what granularity does shipboard revenue land in the Accounting Hub?

Every dollar of onboard revenue reaches the ledger through this interface. Summarising reduces volume and cost but destroys the detail needed to investigate reconciliation variances and answer audit queries years later.

Transaction level — Full investigative and audit capability; highest volume across the fleet.
Daily summary by revenue centre and voyage — Manageable volume, supports voyage reconciliation, loses individual folio traceability.
Voyage-level summary — Lowest volume; makes meaningful reconciliation and audit response effectively impossible.
Our recommendation
Daily summary by revenue centre and voyage, with transaction detail retained in SPMS and retrievable

The ledger does not need every folio line, but it does need enough granularity to reconcile by voyage and revenue stream. Retaining transaction detail in SPMS with a documented retrieval path satisfies audit without loading the ledger.

DD-11

Are onboard concessions presented gross or net, and how is that determined per contract?

Spa, retail, photography and some dining are frequently operated by concessionaires under differing revenue-share terms. Treatment varies by contract and materially affects reported onboard revenue.

Gross with concession cost — Higher reported onboard revenue; requires NCLH to control the service.
Net commission only — Reflects an agency relationship; lower reported revenue.
Per contract assessment — Technically correct, requires a documented assessment for every concession agreement.
Our recommendation
Per contract assessment, documented once at design and re-tested at each contract renewal

Concession terms genuinely differ, so a blanket policy would be wrong for some. The discipline is doing the assessment once per contract and recording it, rather than leaving it to whoever configures the revenue stream.

DD-12

Is travel agent commission netted against receivables, or settled gross?

Agencies both owe for bookings and are owed commission. Netting simplifies settlement and reduces cash movement, but the ledger must still present gross AR and AP for disclosure.

Net settlement with gross ledger presentation — Operationally efficient and matches agency expectation; requires careful configuration to preserve gross reporting.
Gross settlement — Simplest accounting, doubles cash movement and conflicts with how agencies expect to be settled.
Our recommendation
Net settlement with gross ledger presentation

Netting is how the trade actually operates and fighting it creates friction with a channel that originates a large share of bookings. The requirement is that netting is a settlement mechanism, not an accounting shortcut.

Capital

DD-13

How deeply are vessels componentized in the fixed asset register?

Componentization depth determines whether replaced components can be retired at drydock. Too shallow and retirement is impossible, overstating assets and understating depreciation. Too deep and the register becomes unmanageable.

Vessel as a small number of major components — Manageable register, but drydock retirement becomes an estimate rather than a calculation.
Component level aligned to drydock work packages — Retirement becomes mechanical; register size is significant but proportionate.
Deep componentization to equipment level — Maximum precision, register size and maintenance effort become a burden in themselves.
Our recommendation
Component level aligned to drydock work packages

The purpose of componentization is to make drydock capitalization and retirement calculable. Aligning the asset structure to the way drydock work is actually scoped is what makes that work, and it sets the target for the newbuild WBS per DD-14.

DD-14

Does the newbuild WBS follow the shipyard's construction breakdown or the asset component structure?

A newbuild accumulates years of cost that must eventually become component assets. If the WBS does not map to the component structure, capitalization at delivery becomes a large manual allocation exercise under time pressure.

Follow shipyard breakdown — Easier project control against the contract; forces manual allocation at capitalization.
Follow asset component structure — Capitalization becomes mechanical; requires mapping the shipyard's claims into the WBS.
Dual structure with mapping — Both views available; highest setup effort and ongoing maintenance.
Our recommendation
Asset component structure, with a documented mapping from the shipyard breakdown

Construction management can work from a mapping. Accounting cannot manufacture component detail that was never collected. Designing the WBS for the endpoint rather than the process is what makes delivery capitalization a load rather than a project.

DD-15

What is the drydock capital versus expense threshold, and when is it applied?

Drydock combines regulatory survey work, maintenance and refurbishment. The split between capital and expense is a recurring audit focus and, applied inconsistently between drydocks, destroys fleet comparability.

Classify after the event from final cost — How most organisations do it; produces inconsistency between drydocks and different teams.
Pre-classify work packages at planning — Consistency by construction; requires the classification decision at scope definition, months earlier.
Our recommendation
Pre-classify every work package at drydock planning, with change control on reclassification

This is the single largest available improvement to drydock accounting and it costs nothing but discipline. Classifying at planning also forces the capital/expense conversation while the scope can still be shaped.

DD-16

What is the capitalized interest basis for vessels under construction?

Newbuilds take years and involve very large progressive outlays, so capitalized interest is material to carrying value. Fusion does not calculate it, so both the methodology and the mechanism are design decisions.

Specific borrowing rate where export credit financing is tied to the vessel — Most faithful where vessel-specific financing exists; requires tracking financing to the asset.
Weighted average general borrowing rate — Simpler and uniform; may understate or overstate where specific financing exists.
Hybrid — specific where identifiable, general otherwise — Technically correct, needs a documented methodology and consistent application.
Our recommendation
Hybrid, with the methodology documented and applied consistently across all newbuilds

Cruise newbuilds are frequently financed with vessel-specific export credit facilities alongside general corporate debt. A single-basis answer would be wrong for one or the other. What matters most is that the methodology is contemporaneous and unchanging, because this is always tested.

DD-17

Does depreciation commence at delivery or at entry into revenue service?

A newbuild is typically delivered some weeks before its first revenue voyage, and vessels return from drydock before resuming service. The gap is material on an asset of this size.

At delivery — Asset is available for use on acceptance; simplest and most conservative.
At first revenue voyage — Matches depreciation to revenue but defers expense and is harder to defend if the vessel was genuinely ready.
Our recommendation
At delivery, on the basis that the asset is available for use on acceptance

Available for use is the test, not revenue-generating. Delivery voyages, crew familiarisation and positioning are use of the asset. Depreciating from delivery is the more defensible position and avoids an argument about readiness.

Cost

DD-18

Is voyage profitability reported on a fully absorbed basis, or contribution only?

Fully absorbed voyage P&L allocates fleet and corporate overhead down to individual voyages. Allocated cost can exceed directly controllable cost, at which point operators dismiss the whole report as meaningless.

Fully absorbed — Complete economic picture, but attributes cost that voyage-level management cannot influence.
Contribution only — Focuses on controllable performance; does not show whether a voyage covers its share of the fixed base.
Both, layered — Contribution as the management measure, absorbed as the economic view, presented on one statement.
Our recommendation
Both, layered — contribution reported above the line, absorbed below

The two measures answer different questions and forcing a choice loses one of them. Layering also defuses the allocation-basis argument, because operators are measured on the line they control while the executive view retains full absorption.

Treasury

DD-19

Where is hedge accounting performed for fuel and FX?

Fusion does not deliver hedge accounting. Bunker fuel is a large, volatile cost and a headline earnings sensitivity. Designation, effectiveness testing and documentation must live somewhere with an audit trail.

Treasury management system, interfaced to Fusion — Purpose-built for designation, valuation and effectiveness; adds a system and a licence.
Bespoke process feeding Fusion via Accounting Hub — Avoids a new system; puts a material and heavily audited process into custom build.
Spreadsheets with manual journals — Cheapest and how many organisations operate today; weakest audit trail on a material disclosure.
Our recommendation
Treasury management system, interfaced to Fusion

Hedge accounting failures are usually documentation failures, and documentation discipline is what a purpose-built system enforces. Given the size of the fuel exposure and the visibility of the disclosure, this is the wrong place to economise.

Operations

DD-20

Is bonded stores tracked in Fusion Inventory or a specialist system?

Bonded stock attracts duty when landed or sold in territorial waters, must be sealed under customs supervision, and is inspected without notice in every jurisdiction the vessel enters. Discrepancies are treated as potential smuggling, not ordinary stock loss.

Fusion Inventory with a bonded subinventory — One system, but Fusion has no concept of customs seal status or jurisdiction-triggered duty.
Specialist bonded stores system interfaced to Fusion — Purpose-built for seal management and declarations; another system to run.
Fusion with an extension for seal status and duty triggers — Keeps one system of record; the extension carries regulatory consequence if it is wrong.
Our recommendation
Fusion Inventory for stock and value, with seal status and declarations in a specialist capability

The financial inventory position belongs in Fusion. The customs compliance layer is a regulatory function with penalties attached, and building it as an ERP extension puts regulatory exposure into a customisation nobody will maintain.

DD-21

Is bunker procurement and BDN reconciliation built in Fusion or retained in a bunker system?

Bunker involves index-linked pricing, quantity measurement adjusted for temperature and density, sealed sampling, and a formal note-of-protest dispute process with tight contractual deadlines. None of it is a standard Fusion pattern.

Fusion Purchasing with an extension — One system, but the extension must model BDN reconciliation and the dispute lifecycle.
Specialist bunker management system interfaced to Fusion — Purpose-built; Fusion receives the purchase and inventory result.
Fusion for the transaction, manual BDN reconciliation — Lowest build; leaves the highest-value control as a manual process.
Our recommendation
Specialist bunker capability interfaced to Fusion for purchase, inventory and cost

Short delivery and off-specification fuel are documented industry problems with real financial consequence, and the controls that catch them are time-critical at the point of bunkering. That belongs in a system built for it, with Fusion carrying the financial result.

DD-22

Is Concur retained, or replaced with Fusion Expenses?

NCLH has used Concur since 2017. Retaining means building and owning an integration; replacing means change management across the whole employee base plus crew travel, during an already large programme.

Retain Concur — Preserves the existing investment and user familiarity; requires master data sync and expense posting integration.
Replace with Fusion Expenses — One fewer system and licence, native integration; adds significant change scope to the programme.
Our recommendation
Retain Concur for this programme; revisit at the next licence renewal

Replacing a working expense system mid-transformation adds change load for a benefit that can be realised later at lower risk. The integration is well-trodden. The one dependency to manage carefully is that the COA redesign invalidates every Concur cost object mapping at cutover.

DD-26

Does crew payroll stay in PeopleSoft HCM, and how is it attributed to vessels?

NCLH remains on PeopleSoft HCM. Payroll is a large expense line, and crew rotate between vessels mid-period, so attribution must follow the assignment rather than the contract or the vessel-level cost picture is wrong.

PeopleSoft HCM retained, interfaced to Fusion GL — Matches the stated Finance-only scope; requires a payroll-to-GL interface rebuilt against the new COA.
Fusion HCM Payroll in scope — Single vendor and native posting; expands a Finance programme into HCM with very different change and compliance load.
Our recommendation
Retain PeopleSoft HCM with a rebuilt payroll interface; attribute crew cost by vessel assignment, not contract

The RFP scope is Finance, and multi-flag, multi-nationality crew payroll is among the most complex HCM problems there is — not something to absorb into a finance transformation. The attribution rule matters more than the platform: if crew cost follows the contract rather than the assignment, every vessel cost report is quietly wrong.

Migration

DD-23

How much history migrates, and what happens to PeopleSoft afterwards?

There is no upgrade path from PeopleSoft to Fusion. History migration scope is the largest single driver of migration effort, and without a decommissioning plan PeopleSoft runs at full cost indefinitely purely for historical access.

Balances only, plus comparatives — Lowest effort and risk; historical detail requires legacy access.
Two to three years of transactional history — Supports most operational lookback; substantial extra effort and reconciliation.
Full history — Rarely affordable and rarely used enough to justify the cost.
Our recommendation
Balances plus comparatives into Fusion, with PeopleSoft retained read-only for the statutory retention period

Business requests for full history are near-universal and almost never justified by actual usage. A read-only legacy instance with a documented retrieval path meets audit and retention at a fraction of the migration cost — but it must be planned, funded and time-boxed, or it becomes permanent.

DD-24

How long is the parallel run, and what are the acceptance criteria?

The parallel run is the principal assurance that Fusion produces equivalent results before PeopleSoft is retired. It doubles the workload for a finance team already carrying the programme, so it is the first thing cut when the schedule slips.

One period full parallel — Minimum credible assurance; a single close may not exercise every scenario.
Two to three periods — Exercises quarter-end and more transaction variety; heavy resource load.
Targeted parallel on high-risk areas only — Lower load; leaves untested areas that go live unproven.
Our recommendation
Two periods, one of which must be a quarter end, with acceptance criteria agreed in writing before the parallel starts

A single month-end will not exercise consolidation, reporting and quarter-end processes. The more important half of this decision is fixing acceptance criteria in advance — criteria written after results are seen get adjusted to fit the results.

DD-25

How are EU ETS allowances and FuelEU Maritime costs accounted for?

Maritime is inside the EU Emissions Trading System, phasing to full scope, with FuelEU Maritime running alongside. This creates a real and growing P&L and balance sheet item — allowance purchase, liability accrual as emissions occur, and surrender — with no standard Fusion treatment.

Treat allowances as intangible assets with a liability accrued as emissions occur — The prevailing approach in other ETS-covered sectors; requires an accrual model driven by voyage emissions data.
Expense allowances on purchase — Simplest; mismatches cost against the emissions that create the obligation.
Defer pending further guidance — Leaves a growing liability unrecognised and unmodelled.
Our recommendation
Allowances as intangible assets with liability accrued on emissions, and carbon cost modelled inside fuel economics

The obligation arises as the voyage emits, not when allowances are bought, so accrual on emissions is the faithful treatment. Just as important commercially: carbon cost effectively raises the fuel price on EU itineraries and should sit inside fuel scenario planning rather than beside it.
